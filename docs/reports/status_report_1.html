1. Summary

The data pipeline, evaluation harness, and frontier response cache are built and validated. All splits are deduplicated and frozen. The week-1 feasibility pilot passed its go/no-go check, and full baselines now exist for every evaluation item (5,409 items across MedMCQA calibration, MedMCQA test, and MedQA). They cover the unmodified Qwen3-8B in bf16 and in NF4, and the frontier model, Claude Opus 5.5.

Three findings shape the rest of the study. (i) There is ample escalation headroom. On the MedMCQA test set the frontier answers correctly on 34–37% of items that the small model gets wrong, and an oracle cascade would beat the frontier on its own (0.925 vs. 0.890). (ii) The base model is already severely overconfident. Mean confidence is 0.92 against 56% accuracy, and ECE is about 0.35. H1 therefore has to be framed as measuring the direction of change. (iii) Both confidence signals rank items only moderately well (AUROC ≈ 0.73), leaving substantial room for fine-tuning and recalibration to move the cascade. QLoRA training code is complete and under a smoke test; fine-tuning seed 1 is the next run.

Accuracy CIs on the test set are about ±1.8 points. Budget-regime accuracies are expected values with ties broken at random. At a 30% call rate on the NF4 model, escalating random items would give about 0.66 and perfect routing about 0.86.

  • Quantization alone matters. NF4 lowers accuracy by 1.5–2.3 points, raises ECE by 0.011, and lowers AUROC by about 0.01. All before/after-fine-tuning comparisons will therefore be made against the NF4 base, and the bf16–NF4 difference will be reported on its own.
  • The two signals rank items differently. By AUROC, the option-probability signal ranks the whole set slightly better. At call rates of 10–50%, which depend only on the least-confident items, permutation agreement gives the better cascade. This complementarity is what the two-feature logistic combiner can exploit.
  • Frontier answers were clean. Every response parsed except two MedQA items the frontier refused; those are scored as incorrect (2 of 1,272).

6. Implications for the hypotheses

  • H1 (fine-tuning raises ECE). The base model's confidence is already near 1.0 on most items. If fine-tuning raises accuracy while confidence stays saturated, ECE will fall. The proposal's risk table anticipated this; the result is reportable either way, and the threshold regime fails for the base model already.
  • H2 (permutation agreement loses discrimination). The baseline is now fixed: AUROC 0.725 for permutation agreement and 0.729 for option probability (NF4). Permutation agreement is the stronger signal in the budget regime at baseline.
  • H3 (cascade target). The example target, 95% of frontier accuracy (0.846) at a 30% call rate, is far from the base model's 0.714–0.735. Closing that gap is the job of fine-tuning and recalibration.
  • H4 (recalibration). An ECE of about 0.35 leaves a large effect for temperature scaling to repair in the threshold regime. Because the two signals rank items differently, the logistic combiner has room to improve the budget regime.
  • Contamination (§9.1 of the proposal). On MedQA the frontier reaches 0.969 while writing about 50 output tokens per item, against about 110 on MedMCQA. Both models also score higher on MedQA than on MedMCQA. Both patterns fit MedQA's known overlap with web data. MedQA remains a consistency check only.

8. Compute and cost to date

  • Frontier API: about $15 for 5,409 items (about 1.0 M input and 0.52 M output tokens), within the proposal's $10–60 range. No further frontier calls are needed: escalation is simulated offline from the cache.
  • GPU: under 1 H100-hour so far. A full evaluation pass (5,409 items × 10 option orders = 54,090 forward passes) takes about 11 minutes of scoring per model; peak memory is 17 GB in bf16 and 8 GB in NF4.
  • Environment issues, all resolved: PyTorch is pinned to its CUDA 12.8 build (the LARCC drivers support CUDA up to 12.9), the environment is reproducible from requirements.lock, and the setup steps are written down for collaborators.
